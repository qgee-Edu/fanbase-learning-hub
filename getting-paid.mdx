## Lesson 1 -- The Three Ways to Earn on Fanbase

Every dollar you earn on Fanbase comes from one of three sources. Understanding how each one works is the foundation of your Fanbase business.

### Loves (Fan Tips)

Loves are Fanbase's virtual currency. Fans purchase Love packs and send them to you as tips on any post, during a live stream, in an Audio Room, or as a direct reaction to your content.

**How the math works:**

| Love Amount | Earnings |
|---|---|
| 100 Loves | $0.50 |
| 500 Loves | $2.50 |
| 1,000 Loves | $5.00 |
| 2,500 Loves | $12.50 |
| 10,000 Loves | $50.00 |
| 25,000 Loves | $125.00 |

_1 Love = $0.005 (half a penny)_

<Info>
  **Example:** A fan sends you 1,000 Loves during a live stream. That's $5.00 added to your earnings. If 10 fans each send 1,000 Loves during that stream, that's $50.00.
</Info>

---

### Subscriptions

Fans can subscribe to your Fanbase page for a monthly fee. You set the price -- $2.99 to $99.99/month. Subscribers get access to all your exclusive content for as long as they're subscribed.

- You choose your subscription price. Fanbase does not set it for you.
- **Subscription Price Selection** lets you offer multiple price points for fans to choose from.
- Subscription revenue appears in your dashboard monthly as fans' billing cycles renew.
- Exclusive content is automatically unlocked for any active subscriber.

### Direct Content Revenue

In addition to tips and subscriptions, creators earn when fans purchase or unlock content through Love-denominated pricing (for example, paying 125 Loves to unlock a specific Fanbase+ video).

## Lesson 2 -- How Payments Flow

Here's what happens behind the scenes when a fan pays you:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fan purchases Loves or subscribes">
    The transaction happens via the Fanbase app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fanbase processes the payment">
    All transactions run through Stripe, the platform's payment processor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Revenue appears in your Dashboard">
    Earnings are recorded in real time and visible in your Revenue Dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Balance accumulates">
    You can cash out anytime once you hit the $5.00 minimum.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cashout sent via PayPal">
    Funds transfer to your connected PayPal account.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Key payment details:**

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Minimum cashout | $5.00 |
| Payout method | PayPal (must be connected -- see Module 1.2) |
| Processing time | Verify with Lauren/Justin for current timeline |

  **VERIFY:** Confirm current cashout processing time. Confirm whether international creators have different payout options or requirements.

## Lesson 3 -- Reading Your Revenue Dashboard

Your Revenue Dashboard is your financial command center on Fanbase. It shows everything: total balance, earnings by type, and transaction history.

### How to Access Your Revenue Dashboard

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap your Profile icon">
    Bottom right of the navigation bar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap the Settings/gear icon">
    Top right of your profile page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Revenue Dashboard from the menu" />
</Steps>

### What You'll See
![Revenue Dashboard](/images/revenue-dashboard.png)
<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Total Balance" icon="wallet">
    Your current available earnings. This is the amount you can cash out right now.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Earnings Breakdown" icon="chart-pie">
    - **Loves:** Total earnings from fan tips across all content and live sessions
    - **Subscriptions:** Monthly recurring revenue from active subscribers
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Transaction History" icon="list">
    A chronological log of every Love tip, subscription payment, and cashout. You can filter by date range and payment type.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  **What to watch:** Your Loves-to-Subscriptions ratio tells you a lot about your audience. A high Loves number with low subscriptions usually means fans are engaged but haven't committed. That's your signal to create more exclusive content and make a clear "subscribe" ask.
</Tip>
